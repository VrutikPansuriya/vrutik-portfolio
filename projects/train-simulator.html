Project overview

The project & the goal.

Train Simulator is a rail-focused game experience built around train operation and the supporting systems that make the journey feel alive. My contribution centred on gameplay and simulation features that connect track movement, train components, camera transitions, time-of-day, and contextual audio.

A key part of the work was keeping the train and its connected components moving consistently along the track while exposing useful player feedback and sound cues during operation.

My responsibilities: As Gameplay Programmer, I implemented and refined train movement and simulation behaviour, bogie motion, camera-mode transitions, day/night flow, and train-related audio events.

Work breakdown

Systems I worked on.

01

Spline-based train movement

Implemented train movement along a track spline using distance-based positioning, with speed control and movement updates designed to keep the train aligned to the rail path.

02

Bogie movement & train composition

Worked on bogie motion so the connected train components follow the route smoothly, including handling movement behaviour across track segments.

03

Camera transitions

Implemented camera-mode flow for the player when moving between on-foot exploration and train operation, supporting the different viewpoints required by each state.

04

Day/night cycle

Built a timeline-driven day/night cycle to transition the environment over time and support the changing atmosphere during a journey.

05

Contextual train audio

Integrated train-related audio events, including door opening and closing, announcements, engine start, idle and moving sounds, and station-arrival cues.

06

Simulation feedback

Worked on supporting player-facing feedback for train operation and the overall simulation flow, connecting gameplay state with readable in-game information.

Engineering notes

Connecting simulation systems

The main technical challenge in a rail simulation is making multiple systems agree on the same journey state. Train position, bogie motion, player camera state, audio cues, and environmental time changes need to work together so that the experience feels coherent to the player.

The emphasis throughout was on building gameplay features that are understandable, testable, and dependable in a playable build.
