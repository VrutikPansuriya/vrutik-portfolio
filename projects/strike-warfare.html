Project overview

The project & the goal.

Strike Warfare: Battle Fury is a tactical first-person shooter developed by a small team. My work focused on turning the game’s core design requirements into reliable gameplay systems, from weapon handling and damage logic to enemy behaviour and player-facing UI.

Alongside feature development, I contributed to content optimization, debugging, localization support, platform integrations, and build preparation. The work covered both in-game systems and the practical technical steps required to prepare builds for distribution.

My responsibilities: As a Gameplay Programmer, I contributed to implementing and refining gameplay systems across weapons, damage logic, enemy behaviour, UI, optimization, and platform integration.

Work breakdown

Systems I worked on.

01

Weapon handling & combat

Implemented weapon gameplay including weapon slots, pistol handling, reload flow, and weapon-specific data. Worked on firing and damage logic for different weapon behaviours.

02

Damage model & hit detection

Built line-trace based bullet hit detection, hit-zone handling for headshots, and radial damage behaviour for explosive weapons such as the rocket launcher.

03

Enemy behaviour & gameplay logic

Worked on enemy AI behaviour and perception-driven gameplay, connecting combat encounters with the wider gameplay loop.

04

UI & player feedback

Implemented gameplay-facing UI and menus, helping communicate weapon state, player information, and game flow to the player.

05

Optimization & production support

Worked on draw-call and content optimization, alongside debugging, packaging, and production support.

06

Platform integration & delivery

Worked with Steam, Epic Online Services, Microsoft Store/MSIX, achievement and trophy workflows, and PC/console build preparation.

Engineering notes

Technical delivery beyond gameplay

The project also involved practical release engineering tasks: storefront and online-service integration, achievement setup, package validation, build troubleshooting, and localization support. This gave me experience following gameplay work through to platform-specific delivery rather than stopping at the editor.

The emphasis throughout was on building gameplay features that are understandable, testable, and dependable in a playable build.
